# Reproducible methods

MSIpath ranks normal reference donors using ancestry-marker and flanking-SNP likelihoods, retains 20 donors (up to 40 haplotypes), then infers local copying paths and marginal genotype weights. SNP evidence alone selects the reference. STR observations do not choose a more favorable reference.

At a locus, the null is a genotype- and allelic-balance-weighted normal length model. The alternative changes one allele by one fixed shift shared across observations, marginalizing genotype, balance, altered allele, shift and altered-molecule fraction once per locus. Expansion and contraction directions receive equal prior mass. The fixed shift distribution mixes 0.7 short-geometric and 0.3 broad-geometric mass over 1–40 repeat units. The fraction prior is a truncated Beta(1,9) integrated by 12-point quadrature, bounded by the selected allele's mass and 0.5. The observation model retains mapping uncertainty. Only exact STR fragments enter the scored likelihood, with at least 3 exact and 5 total fragments per eligible locus.

The sample score is the sum of locus log Bayes factors clipped to [0,10], requiring at least 5 eligible loci. Independent normal ranks give `(1 + number of normal scores >= query score)/(N+1)`. The fixed alpha is 0.05. A query without enough evidence is UNCALLABLE; a scored query without a calibration bank is UNCALIBRATED. There is no GATES integration, locus Monte Carlo test or BH gate in the released decision rule.

The bundled normal error profiles were fitted from 40 reference-only donors using MAP normal-genotype residual categories and pooled shrinkage. They are phenomenological residual parameters, not measured biochemical stutter rates. These parameters and all model priors were frozen before the full evaluation. The bundle supplies the profiles and donor provenance; it does not claim that these profiles are transferable to an unrelated assay.

The study simulates PCR-free PE150 reads from chr19/chr22 target regions, with 200 selected STR loci, phased SNP/STR templates, ART sequencing errors, and 30x primary coverage. The robustness study includes 10x/20x/30x/60x. Tumor purity p is defined under clonal heterozygous alterations in diploid copy-neutral cells, so altered-molecule fraction equals p/2. b is the fraction of altered loci. The other loci can remain stable even in a tumor sample; b and p are distinct parameters.

Calibration and performance summaries use same-depth independent normal cohorts. Native tool calls and frozen-30x calibration are retained as secondary columns. AUROC/AUPRC compare each positive condition against ordinary normal samples; difficult normal challenges have separate false-positive results. Confidence intervals use donor-level resampling or Wilson binomial intervals as specified in the tables. Multiple perturbations of the same donor are not independent patients.

Resource measurements include fresh process startup, prebuilt model loading, BAM evidence extraction, inference, calibration and output. They exclude common alignment, simulation, one-time model/baseline training and output archiving. Each method processes the same 10 tumor/normal-state samples from 5 donors, with 3 fresh-process repeats and one CPU affinity. MANTIS2 additionally consumes matched normal BAMs. All five methods share 15 supported loci; a separate 200-locus comparison omits MSIsensor2. Score/callable-locus agreement was checked for all 270 executions. The released package is a refactoring of the measured algorithm; its wrapper/file-output costs have not been separately rebenchmarked.

Limitations: the simulated rare/novel-normal edits preserve SNP background and can disrupt natural linkage; results are stress tests, not empirical population prevalence. Rare and unseen inherited STR alleles still produce extensive false positives. Development and evaluation donors overlap; these are expanded reused-data experiments, not independent clinical validation. The weakest purity and low-depth settings remain challenging. The published resource sample is small and retains uncallable executions rather than dropping them.
